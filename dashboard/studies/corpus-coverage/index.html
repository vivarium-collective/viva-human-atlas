Question & approach

Of the HRA ASCT+B-3D crosswalk's 1,730 Uberon-keyed anatomical structures (across 114 source-organ GLBs), how many are covered once every curated BioModels model — not just a narrow search query — is crossed against them, and does full-corpus coverage reach organs a single-query search misses (e.g. kidney)?
Summary. A single BioModels search query (as in `model-coverage-3d`) only ever samples the corpus through the lens of its search terms. This study instead retrieves and organ-tags the full curated BioModels catalog (1,096 `BIOMD*` models, Task A) and crosses every organ it can tag against the ASCT+B-3D crosswalk, giving the ceiling on how much of the HRA's fine-grained 3D anatomy the *entire* published mechanistic-modeling literature (as indexed by BioModels) actually touches — not just the glucose-regulation slice. This directly measures how much anatomy is "dark" (unmodeled) versus "lit" (has at least one curated model), informing where new HRA-3D coverage work would have the most impact.

🔬 Findings — what this study taught us

◆ Crossing the full 1,096-model curated BioModels catalog (82/1,096, 7.5%, organ-tagged) against the ASCT+B-3D crosswalk covers 635 AS nodes (36.7%) across 31 distinct organ GLBs (27.2%) -- an order of magnitude wider than the 25-result glucose-regulation query's 58 AS nodes / 4 organs, and critically newly reaches kidney, confirming full-corpus retrieval finds anatomically relevant models a single search term misses entirely.

Limitations

What this study explicitly does NOT validate (prevents overclaim).

  • 92.5% of the corpus (1,014/1,096) is untagged -- either genuinely organ-agnostic (pathway/signaling models with no single anatomical site) or organ-specific but missed by the current synonym/reference-organ-name matching.
  • Coverage is still organ-granularity, not per-AS-node model matching: a covered organ's Uberon marks every AS node cut from its GLB as covered, which can overstate coverage for anatomically distinct sub-structures with no direct model evidence.
